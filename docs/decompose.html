On court rtg
His on-court rating: points per 100 possessions his team scored minus allowed while he was on the court. Only those possessions: nothing from when he sits. The starting point.
Off court rtg
Actual: the same with him off the court, in games he played. Shown for comparison; not a piece.
Teammates
Minus the combined RAPM of the four teammates on the court with him, weighted by time together. Strong teammates make it negative: they account for part of the margin.
Opponents
The combined RAPM of the five opponents on the court with him, weighted by time. Strong opponents make it positive.
Context
Minus what home court, playoffs, garbage time, the score margin and the season predict for his minutes.
Ridge penalty
The regression's pull toward zero, which keeps small samples from producing extreme ratings. Negative for good players, and larger the fewer possessions he has.

Each piece is exactly how much his RAPM would move if that group of possessions had come out average for their situation. Context and the ridge penalty are not pieces here: context is taken out of every possession, and the penalty sets how much each possession counts. On court rtg and Off court rtg are the actual ratings, shown for comparison; they are not pieces.

On court rtg
Actual: points per 100 possessions his team scored minus allowed with him on the court.
Off court rtg
Actual: the same with him off the court, in games he played.
On court credit
The part of his RAPM that comes from his own possessions: his team's results with him on the court, measured against what the situation predicts, after each possession's credit is shared with the other nine players on the court.
Off court adjustment (GP)
How his team did without him, in games he played. A team that falls apart without him adds to his rating; one that stays good takes away.
Off court adjustment (DNP)
The same for his team's games he didn't play (injury, rest or coach's decision), in seasons he was on the team. A season missed entirely can't be placed, because injured players aren't in the box scores, so it counts under Team SOS adjustment.
Team SOS adjustment
Games his team wasn't in: his teammates' games for other teams, his opponents' games against everyone else, and his team in seasons he wasn't on it. They move his rating only through how good the players around him look: if his opponents prove strong elsewhere, he gets more credit for beating them.
RAPM
Vanilla regularized adjusted plus-minus on points: regular season and playoffs, fit on the chosen span alone, ridge penalty 3,000 per side. Unlike the main ratings, it has no box-score prior.
Why both splits add up: by player, the five pieces are the regression's own equation for each player, and each averages zero across the league, weighted by possessions. By possession, RAPM is a weighted sum of every possession's result, so grouping the possessions splits it exactly. Every row adds up to his RAPM to rounding. Teammates' and opponents' ratings come from the same fit. Source (scripts/83_decompose_site.py).
